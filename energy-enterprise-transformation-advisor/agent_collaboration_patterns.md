# Agent Collaboration Patterns

## Purpose and Evidence

This document describes how the 19 domain agents collaborate across the Energy Enterprise Transformation Advisor. It synthesizes the generated per-agent `SKILL.md` files, agent READMEs/sample-data dictionaries, `Agent_Interaction_Model.md`, `enterprise_process_model.md`, `agent_capability_matrix.md`, master-data documentation, capability/KPI models, and `sap_reference_architecture.md`.

The collaboration model is a design/reference view, not evidence of deployed integrations. The enterprise process model catalogs 28 named handoffs; the Agent Interaction Model defines only three explicit input/event contracts into Supply Planning. Skill/README interactions add useful relationships but often lack event payload, owner, SLA, and cadence. Below, `H-##` means a handoff in the process model; `AIM` means a contract in the Agent Interaction Model; `Coord.` means documented coordination without a defined interface. A few skill-level links cross the formal process-model direction; these are described as advisory or coordination rather than silently treated as formal events.

**Direction:** Upstream agents provide data, constraints, or decisions consumed by the row agent. Downstream agents consume its output. Decision dependencies identify approvals, constraints, or evidence needed before the row agent can publish or act.

## 2. End-to-End Business Process Flows

The following are the process model's six defined cross-agent cycles, plus a strategic transformation flow assembled from its ownership/handoff tables. Events below are business triggers; technical event topics/API payloads remain design work unless separately confirmed.

### 2.1 Sales and Operations Planning (monthly)

1. Demand Planning publishes an unconstrained, versioned forecast by product/region/period.
2. Commercial Marketing reviews demand, pricing, product launches and discontinuations; changes trigger H-05 back to Demand Planning.
3. Supply Planning reconciles the approved demand forecast (H-01/AIM) against plant capacity, inventory, procurement lead times, asset status, and external constraints.
4. Supply Planning issues the constrained plan: H-02 to Refining Operations, H-03 safety-stock/reorder targets to Warehouse, H-04 allocations to Logistics; the procurement plan is shared with Procurement (AIM).
5. Finance models revenue, margin, and operating-cost impact; Corporate Strategy chairs executive S&OP and approves exceptions.
6. Warehouse and Logistics execute approved inventory/distribution plans; actuals and constraints feed the next cycle.

**Decision gates:** forecast review; constraint and allocation trade-off; executive plan approval; exception escalation. AIM requires human escalation for forecast variance above 20% and procurement-plan conflicts.

### 2.2 Procure-to-Pay (P2P)

`Warehouse / Operations requisition -> Procurement sourcing and PO -> Warehouse goods receipt -> Procurement three-way match -> Finance payment`

Triggers/data: material need creates a PR; PO placement sends expected delivery/material/quantity to Warehouse (H-22); posted receipt sends GR and quality/quantity variance to Procurement (H-23); approved invoice/match result moves to Finance (H-24). Finance confirms payment and cash impact.

**Decision gates:** supplier qualification and award; delegation-of-authority approval; receipt/quality acceptance; price/quantity/tax match; payment release. Material-master gaps currently prevent reliable end-to-end joins for portions of sample PO and stock data.

### 2.3 Maintenance-to-Reliability (M2R)

`Condition anomaly -> Asset Reliability notification/work order -> Warehouse parts reservation -> HSE risk assessment/PTW -> maintenance execution -> Finance cost posting -> Reliability KPI update`

H-08 moves spare requirements; H-09 requests JHA/PTW; H-10 returns permit clearance and safety conditions; H-11 sends completed work-order cost actuals. Asset status is also a Supply Planning input under AIM; operating agents use health/availability in scheduling.

**Decision gates:** criticality and work priority; parts/labor availability; PTW/isolation approval; return-to-service; repair-versus-replace and deferral approval.

### 2.4 Order-to-Cash (O2C)

`Commercial price/terms -> Sales Trading order/deal -> Trading Risk credit check -> Supply Planning + Warehouse availability/allocation -> Logistics shipment -> Warehouse goods issue -> Finance invoice/collection`

H-12 sends trade details, counterparty, volume and price to Trading Risk; H-13 returns credit approval/decline; H-14 sends confirmed order allocation/delivery date to Warehouse; H-15 sends shipping instruction to Logistics; H-16 sends delivery note/POD to Finance for invoicing. Actual collection and AR clearing remain Finance activities.

**Decision gates:** approved price/contract; credit limit; ATP and allocation; carrier/mode and dangerous-goods route; delivery acceptance and invoice reconciliation.

### 2.5 Incident-to-Close (I2C)

`HSE report and severity triage -> immediate site response with Operations -> RCA/bow-tie -> CAPA owners -> technical/procedural corrective work -> HSE effectiveness verification/closure -> Knowledge Repository lesson`

Incident closure triggers H-21. HSE shares incident/environmental-event data with ESG under H-19. Where equipment failure is implicated, Asset Reliability and Operations supply linked asset/work-order evidence.

**Decision gates:** emergency command and shutdown/isolation; severity/API RP 754 and reportability classification; CAPA owner/due date; effectiveness and closure. Immediate danger takes precedence over agent analysis.

### 2.6 ESG Reporting Cycle

`Upstream/Refining/HSE activity data -> ESG boundary and quality validation -> emissions/water/social calculations -> disclosure review -> strategy and finance sign-off -> analytics dashboard`

Monthly production/flaring/energy data from Upstream (H-17), refinery fuel/emission/effluent data from Refining (H-18), and incidents/environmental events from HSE (H-19) feed ESG calculation. ESG report draft sends sustainability KPIs/submissions to Corporate Strategy (H-20); Data Analytics publishes a governed dashboard.

**Decision gates:** reporting boundary and ownership; emission-factor/GWP/method selection; restatement and assurance; disclosure/legal approval. Synthetic ESG examples and formulas are not production accounting policy.

### 2.7 Strategy-to-Transformation-to-Architecture

`Corporate Strategy priority -> Finance investment case -> Enterprise Architecture target-state decision -> Transformation PMO initiative/stage gates -> domain delivery and benefits -> Strategy portfolio review`

H-06 transfers the strategic investment/CAPEX envelope to Finance; H-07 returns NPV/IRR analysis. H-26 transfers architecture decision/project impact to PMO. H-25 returns milestone status, benefits, and risks to Strategy. Finance validates actual cost and benefit evidence; Knowledge Repository captures decisions and lessons.

**Decision gates:** portfolio prioritization and business case; architecture/security/clean-core approval; stage-gate funding; scope/rebaseline; benefit acceptance and reinvestment/stop decision.

## 4. Escalation Paths

1. **Demand exception:** forecast variance greater than 20% -> human demand planner -> S&OP owner/executive review; preserve forecast version and source evidence.
2. **Supply/procurement conflict:** inconsistent procurement and supply plans -> Supply Planning + Procurement managers -> executive S&OP decision; do not auto-release conflicting plan.
3. **Safety/environmental emergency:** imminent danger, major accident hazard, serious injury, uncontrolled release, permit breach -> site emergency command/HSE immediately -> regulator/legal notification decision by authorized people. Emergency response must not wait for agent output.
4. **Maintenance/permit:** critical asset degradation or work without valid permit/isolation -> Operations/HSE authority; stop or defer work based on site rules, not model confidence alone.
5. **Credit/risk:** credit decline, limit breach, collateral shortfall, model/valuation failure -> Trading Risk leadership and delegated trader/credit approver; prohibit autonomous override or trade release.
6. **Order/logistics:** unavailable inventory, missed critical delivery, unsafe route, dangerous-goods documentation gap, or customs hold -> Operations/Logistics/HSE and commercial owner; reallocation/reroute requires approval.
7. **Finance/procurement control:** unmatched invoice, unauthorized spend, close exception, suspected misstatement -> Procurement/Finance control owner; payment and journal approvals remain segregated.
8. **Data/model/security:** critical-data quality or freshness failure, PII/ACL violation, drift, untraceable answer, or broken production SLA -> source data owner + Data Analytics; Security/Model Risk/EA escalation as applicable.
9. **Transformation/architecture:** stage-gate failure, material rebaseline, unsupported technology, clean-core exception, cyber/OT risk -> program steering committee and Architecture/Security Board.
10. **ESG disclosure:** boundary or methodology uncertainty, restatement, assurance finding, or unsupported claim -> ESG data owner, assurance/legal, Finance and executive disclosure approver.

## 8. Orchestration and Integration Guardrails

1. Keep the master agent responsible for routing and workflow state; domain agents own their domain analysis and source definitions.
2. Prefer event-driven handoffs with a named producer/consumer, business trigger, versioned schema, canonical keys, idempotency key, timestamp, data classification, and SLA. Current Event Mesh topic names in the SAP reference are proposals, not existing topics.
3. Agents read governed semantic data from Datasphere/approved APIs and write only through released SAP APIs or approved workflow actions. No direct table writes or shared ERP credentials.
4. Human approvals remain in the relevant SAP/business workflow: procurement award/payment, trade credit/limit, safety permits, operating changes, financial close, ESG disclosures, architecture exceptions and investment gates.
5. Every KPI, forecast, risk result, and ESG value carries owner, formula/method version, source lineage, period, currency/UoM, status and exception state.
6. Use least-privilege access, respect source ACLs and PII classifications, and preserve audit trails for agent inputs, recommendations, overrides and approvals.
7. Treat the formal process model's handoffs as conceptual until contracts are reconciled with the three AIM contracts, checked for conflicting direction/content, and implemented/tested.
